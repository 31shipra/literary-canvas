Stories that stay.

Fiction about ordinary lives, quiet choices, unexpected moments, and the stories we carry long after the last page.

The Last Letter

The letter arrived on a Tuesday morning, twenty-three years late.

Mira almost threw it away.

There was no return address, only her name written carefully across the envelope in handwriting she had not seen since she was seventeen.

She stood beside the kitchen window for a long time before opening it. Outside, the street was waking slowly. A bicycle passed. Somewhere nearby, a kettle whistled. Ordinary things continued to happen, unaware that the past had just arrived at her doorstep.

Inside was a single sheet of paper.

She read the first sentence twice.

Then she sat down.

Some letters carry news. Some carry apologies. And some simply remind us that there were versions of ourselves we once left behind.

Mira folded the letter carefully and placed it beside her cup of tea.

For the first time in many years, she smiled at the person she used to be.

The Window Seat

Every evening, the old woman sat beside the same window.

No one in the neighborhood knew exactly what she was waiting for.

Perhaps she wasn't waiting at all.

Perhaps she simply liked watching the world return home.

Children ran through the lane. Shopkeepers pulled down their shutters. Couples walked beneath the fading light, carrying groceries and small conversations.

And every evening, when the sky became the color of ash and roses, she opened the window a little wider.

Because sometimes hope is not a dramatic thing.

Sometimes it is simply the decision to open the window again tomorrow.
